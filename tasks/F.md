### F3 — Implement the strict canonical decode layer with a distinct-error taxonomy
- Milestone: M0
- Size: L
- Deps: F1, F2
- Spec: Definitions & encoding — decoder MUST reject non-canonical form (MVP-SPEC.md line 73); Tamper matrix distinct-error requirement (line 168); Milestones M0 (line 153)
- Do: Build `antseal_core::codec::decode`: a canonicality-enforcing reader over the pinned crate (or in-house per F1 contingency) that hard-errors on every rejection class of line 73: duplicate map keys; non-shortest integer encodings; non-shortest length-argument encodings; indefinite-length items (maps, arrays, strings, byte strings); map keys not in strictly-ascending bytewise encoded order (strict ascent rejects duplicates and disorder via two separate variants); floats and out-of-schema simple values; trailing bytes after the top-level item; invalid UTF-8 in tstr. Errors are a `thiserror` enum with one distinct variant per class, carrying position/context but never the byte content of secret-bearing fields. The same layer is invoked on the outer manifest, the outer bundle, the inner `body` bstr contents, and the bundle-embedded manifest bytes. Library code returns errors — no panics, no `unwrap`, on any input.
- Accept:
  - One unit test per rejection class × {outer envelope, inner body} proving the exact distinct variant is returned.
  - Duplicate-key and out-of-order-key produce two different variants.
  - Truncation sweep: every strict prefix (length 0..len−1) of a valid golden encoding returns a typed error, never panics.
  - `decode(encode(x)) == x` and `encode(decode(bytes)) == bytes` for canonical inputs.
  - Error `Display`/`Debug` output contains no field byte content (reviewed test over a secret-bearing fixture).

- Do: teach the checker the other two envelope shapes, so that a `cbor=yes` verdict on one of these kinds is actionable rather than a permanent red. Scope is the **reader**, not the policy: the flag itself, the must-exist biconditional and `manifest-aead`'s recorded reason are all **F31**'s and are not re-litigated here.
- Accept: a case carrying a diagnostic sidecar is checked whichever of the three envelope shapes holds it; each of the five kinds is in scope, or out of scope for a reason that names its *content* rather than its container; and the vacuity guard survives unchanged, since this adds coverage and does not replace a floor.
- Notes: **checked against F31's entry before minting, as D112 asks.** F31's `Do` covers making the two silences distinguishable and making a flagged-but-unreachable kind red — its Accept reads *"a kind that commits CBOR with no sidecar turns the lane red; a kind that commits no CBOR stays out of scope with a different, non-neutral message."* What F31 leaves out is the reader: after F31, a `cbor=yes` kind with an `expect.vectors` envelope is red and **stays** red, because nothing can walk it. D112 says so in terms at §7 — *"making those kinds reachable is F31's R7 third clause, and only for a kind that declares `cbor=yes`"* — and §8.3 records that making `expect.vectors` kinds genuinely checkable *"is unowned by any row I can find."* This row is that residual. It must land **after** F31 rather than before, because F31 is what decides which of the five ever needs reading — `manifest-aead` in particular may be recorded as flagged-not-CBOR with its argument, in which case its envelope never has to be walked at all.
