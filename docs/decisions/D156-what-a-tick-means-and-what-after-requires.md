### R1 — What `after X` requires of X

*Executed by: nobody — this is the rule the rest runs under. Stated so a lane can
apply it without reading this record.*

> **`after X` requires that X's `Do` has landed and that X's own lane owes
> nothing further on it. It does not require `X` to be `[x]`.**
>
> To discharge an `after X` term, name the artifact X's `Do` promised and show it
> in the tree. If X is `[ ]` **and** its `Do` has landed, the term is discharged
> and the reason is written into the successor's row in the act that ticks it.
> If X's `Do` has **not** landed, the term is live and the successor does not
> tick — whatever X's box says.

This is a **statement of what the register has always done**, not a new licence.
Fifteen ticked rows sit behind open predecessors (§1.3); eight of them behind
`Q1`; `M0`, `M1`, `M2` and `M3` all passed that way; and `Q19`'s own registrar
note already spells the reading out — *"the VENUE HALF IS LANDED AND THE ROW DOES
NOT TICK."* The alternative reading makes those fifteen ticks defects and four
passed gates wrong, which no evidence supports.

**Three consequences a lane must carry:**

1. **The box is not the referent, so do not read it as one.** A successor's lane
   reads the predecessor's `Do` and its Notes — never its checkbox alone. This is
   the memory's standing rule (*"read a predecessor's Notes and Accept, never its
   box"*) promoted from folklore to protocol.
2. **`after <continuous row>` is a special case of this rule, not an exception.**
   D154 §2 R10's continuous-row reading (the edge targets the lane's instrument)
   is exactly *"X's `Do` has landed"* for a row whose `Do` is an instrument.
   `Q20 after U31` stands as the precedent it already was.
3. **This does not weaken rule 4; it is what rule 4 already implies** — see R11.
